# v0.7.0 Spacecraft Travel — Scientific Model Report

Classification: **Educational Accuracy**  
Operational use: **Not permitted**

## Purpose

The v0.7 Travel model demonstrates the basic relationship between planetary orbital geometry, launch windows, transfer time, trajectory shape and idealised Delta-v. It is designed for classroom learning and deterministic visualization.

It is not suitable for navigation, launch planning, spacecraft operations, safety decisions or real mission design.

## Installed trajectory model

The baseline interplanetary route is an idealised heliocentric two-body Hohmann transfer.

For departure radius `r1` and destination radius `r2`:

- transfer semi-major axis: `a = (r1 + r2) / 2`;
- transfer time: `t = π × sqrt(a³ / μSun)`;
- circular speed at radius `r`: `vc = sqrt(μSun / r)`;
- transfer speed at radius `r`: `vt = sqrt(μSun × (2/r − 1/a))`.

The engine solves a departure time within approximately one synodic period so that the destination’s modelled arrival direction aligns with the opposite end of the transfer ellipse. A bounded search followed by deterministic refinement minimizes the launch-phase residual.

The visible trajectory is sampled into deterministic points and ends at the modelled destination position. This endpoint anchoring improves educational interception readability but does not turn the simplified model into a precision ephemeris or operational propagator.

## Mission types

### Fly-by

The required budget includes the departure heliocentric impulse plus a correction reserve. No destination insertion impulse is applied.

### Orbiter

The required budget includes the departure impulse, an idealised destination circularization/insertion impulse and a correction reserve.

### Earth orbital rehearsal

Earth uses a deterministic local orbital demonstration anchored to Earth’s modelled heliocentric position. It is not a launch-site, atmospheric-ascent or certified Earth-orbit simulation.

## Simplified fuel model

Advanced fuel simulation compares the calculated idealised Delta-v requirement with a user-selected available Delta-v budget.

It does not calculate:

- propellant mass;
- rocket equation mass fraction;
- dry mass or payload mass;
- engine thrust or specific impulse;
- staging;
- finite burn losses;
- gravity or aerodynamic losses;
- launch-vehicle performance.

Default Basic mode uses unlimited fuel to avoid interrupting the learning flow.

## Supported and rejected route types

### Hohmann Transfer — supported

A minimum-energy two-impulse educational transfer in the installed two-body baseline.

### Direct Transfer — unavailable

A defensible direct intercept requires a Lambert boundary-value solver and a propulsion/time-of-flight model. v0.7 does not approximate or invent this route.

### Gravity Assist — unavailable

A defensible gravity-assist route requires patched-conic or N-body encounter solving, encounter geometry and body-specific fly-by constraints. v0.7 does not approximate or invent this route.

## Mission state model

Mission status is calculated from authoritative simulation time:

- waiting for launch;
- departure burn;
- interplanetary cruise;
- mid-course correction;
- destination approach;
- arrival burn;
- orbit achieved or fly-by complete.

Key events are deterministic fractions of the solved route and can trigger optional auto-pause. The same mission state drives WebGL, Canvas, dashboard, snapshot and standalone output.

## Baseline verification example — Mars Orbiter

Representative deterministic result from the v0.7 regression suite:

- origin: Earth;
- destination: Mars;
- route: Hohmann transfer;
- mission type: Orbiter;
- duration: approximately **244.1327247 days**;
- required simplified Delta-v: approximately **4.9008018 km/s**;
- launch-phase residual: approximately **6.11 × 10⁻¹⁰ degrees**;
- trajectory samples: **181**;
- educational transfer-path endpoint residual: approximately **0.000351 AU**.

The phase residual measures convergence inside the installed model. It is not an absolute error comparison against an external high-precision trajectory dataset.

## Main simplifications and limitations

- The Sun is the only gravitational primary in interplanetary transfer calculations.
- Planetary perturbations and N-body effects are omitted.
- Orbital planes are not optimized with a real three-dimensional plane-change solution.
- Burn duration is instantaneous.
- Launch-site latitude, Earth rotation, atmosphere and ascent are omitted.
- Parking-orbit escape and sphere-of-influence transitions are omitted.
- Arrival uses idealised circularization rather than body-specific capture dynamics.
- No spacecraft mass, propulsion, communication, power, thermal, radiation or instrument system is modelled.
- No uncertainty propagation, covariance, navigation measurement or correction estimation is provided.
- Planet states come from the installed educational astronomy provider rather than a high-precision operational ephemeris.
